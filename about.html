GMOttomotive

We build the layer under the bonnet.

GMOttomotive makes tools and web apps for the automotive trade. CarDNA is the first of them: a vehicle-identity engine that starts with a VIN and ends with the part that actually fits.

Why

Parts data is broken in a boring way

Every parts site asks the same question — what car is this? — and then answers it badly. You pick a year, a model, a trim from a dropdown that was never quite right, and you hope. The part arrives. It does not fit.

A VIN already contains the answer. Every modern vehicle carries its own configuration in seventeen characters. CarDNA reads it first, then shows only what applies to that exact car — which is a different product from a catalogue with a search box on top.

How

Licensed, sourced, traceable

Vehicle identity comes from public registries — NHTSA in the US, DVSA and DVLA in the UK, EEA in Europe. Catalogues and diagrams come from licences: TecAlliance for the aftermarket, per-manufacturer EPC programmes for OEM drawings.

We do not mirror other people's sites. Every row in the database carries its source, so when a part is wrong we can trace why — and when a licence expires, we know exactly what to remove.

Principles

Five rules we do not bend

01

Identity is free

Decoding a VIN is not a premium feature. It is the price of admission to owning a car.

02

No scraped mirrors

If we do not have a licence for it, we do not have it. That includes diagrams and prices.

03

Every row has a source

Provenance is stored next to the data, not in someone's memory of where it came from.

04

Say what you don't know

If a VIN is not in the registry, we say so instead of guessing from a pattern.

05

No dark patterns

No fake countdowns, no hidden renewals, no masking data you already paid for.

Built to be read

Plain language, real numbers, and a design that does not need a tutorial.

Roadmap

What ships next

  1. Now
    Vehicle identity, free

    VIN decoding, the full make and model index, shareable links. This is what you are looking at.

  2. Next
    Garage and saved vehicles

    Save a car once, then every search is scoped to it. No re-entering a year and trim. The account-backed version runs on a Postgres database where each row is readable only by the account that owns it.

  3. Then
    First catalogue licences

    Aftermarket breadth through TecDoc, plus the first OEM diagram sets for the brands with the deepest demand.

  4. Later
    Fitment, supersessions and prices

    The graph that answers "does this fit my car", with retailer feeds behind the price.

  5. After that
    Workshop tools

    Procedures, torque data, job cards — and an assistant grounded in licensed data rather than guesses.
